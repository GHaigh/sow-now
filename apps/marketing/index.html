The hardware

Standard sensors. Off-the-shelf parts.

Sow Now uses Ecowitt sensors — a widely available, well-supported range you can buy yourself from Amazon, eBay, or direct from Ecowitt, or purchase pre-configured as part of one of our kits. No proprietary hardware, no lock-in. Our recommended starting setup for a typical greenhouse garden is listed below.

  • 🌤

    1 × Ecowitt WS69 — outdoor weather station

    One per site. Mount outside on a post or fence, clear of walls and overhangs. Reads air temperature, humidity, wind speed, rain, and UV. This is the foundation of your garden GDD calculation — one WS69 covers your whole plot.

    Garden zone · 1 per site
  • 🏡

    2 × Ecowitt WH31 — temperature & humidity sensors

    We recommend starting with two WH31s. Channel 1 goes in the greenhouse — hang it out of direct sunlight to track greenhouse GDD separately from the garden (your greenhouse is often 3–6 weeks ahead). Channel 2 sits on the indoor windowsill where you germinate seedlings — the environment there is warmer again, and your seeds will tell you when they're actually ready to move, not just when the calendar says so.

    Greenhouse zone Indoor seedling zone
  • 💧

    2 × Ecowitt WH51 — soil moisture sensors

    We recommend starting with two WH51s. Push one probe into a planter in the greenhouse and one into a bed in the garden. These two locations have very different watering needs — the greenhouse planter dries out faster and the garden bed holds moisture longer. The app tracks each one independently and tells you exactly which needs water and when. Add more sensors as you expand — they're available separately.

    Greenhouse planter + garden bed
  • 📡

    Sow Now Hub — on a windowsill

    Place on any windowsill facing the garden, plugged into USB-C. The built-in radio receives all sensors simultaneously at 868 MHz — no wires, no gateway, up to 60 m range.

    Receives all zones

Why GDD matters

Plants don't care what month it is.
They care how warm it's been.

A tomato sown on 1st March doesn't know it's March. It knows how many warm hours it's experienced. That's what Growing Degree Days measure — accumulated warmth above the temperature at which a plant starts growing. Two gardens 400 miles apart can have completely different growing seasons, even if they plant on the same date.

At 1,380 GDD for the season, a Surrey garden comfortably hits the 1,200 GDD target by mid-August. Sow indoors in March, transplant in May, pick from late July.

Cumbria grower — tight but possible

At 910 GDD outdoors, hitting 1,200 GDD in the garden alone is borderline. But a greenhouse in Cumbria might add 300+ GDD. Sow Now tells you exactly when your greenhouse has reached the threshold — not a calendar guess.

Aberdeen grower — greenhouse essential

720 GDD outdoors won't ripen most tomatoes. But a decent south-facing greenhouse in Aberdeen can accumulate 1,100–1,300 GDD. Sow Now tracks your actual greenhouse, not an average — and adjusts the harvest prediction as the season unfolds.

The postcode forecast problem

Even in the same postcode, a south-facing walled garden can accumulate 200+ more GDD than a north-facing plot. The only way to know is to measure. That's what the WS69 is for.

These are regional estimates, not your garden. The whole point of Sow Now is that your sensors measure your microclimate — the south-facing wall, the sheltered raised bed, the greenhouse that traps heat differently to your neighbour's. Regional averages are where you start. Your sensors are where you finish.

The science

Growing Degree Days — what farms have used for decades

Professional growers have used GDD to predict crop development since the 1950s. It's the accumulated heat energy above a crop's base temperature — a far more accurate predictor of plant development than a calendar date.

The difference is that Sow Now uses your actual sensor readings — not a weather forecast API. Your south-facing greenhouse wall might be accumulating GDD twice as fast as your garden. No weather service can tell you that.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need to buy a separate Ecowitt gateway?
No — the Sow Now hub contains a built-in radio receiver that reads your Ecowitt sensors directly over 868 MHz. No Ecowitt gateway needed, and your data never touches Ecowitt's servers.
Can I use sensors I already own, or buy them myself?
Yes — and that's deliberate. Ecowitt sensors (WS69, WH51, WH31) are standard, widely available hardware you can buy from Amazon, eBay, or directly from Ecowitt as well as from us. There's no proprietary lock-in: if you already own compatible Ecowitt sensors, they'll work straight away. Support for additional Fine Offset compatible sensors is on the roadmap.
What happens if my broadband goes down?
The Sow Now hub stores up to 30 days of sensor readings locally. When your connection returns, all readings are automatically uploaded — no data is lost. Your app will show "Hub last seen X hours ago" if the hub goes offline.
How long do the sensor batteries last?
The Ecowitt WH51 soil sensors and WH31 greenhouse sensor run on AA batteries. At a 48-second transmission interval you can expect 12–18 months of battery life. The app notifies you when a sensor drops below 15%.
How far can the sensors be from the hub?
The Ecowitt sensors transmit on 868 MHz with a quoted range of 100 m in open field. Through walls and fences real-world range is typically 30–60 m — more than enough for most gardens. Place the hub on a windowsill facing the garden for best results.
Is my data private?
Your sensor data is stored under your account only — no other user can see it. We store only your postcode prefix (e.g. "SW4") for climate zone lookup, never your full address. You can delete your account and all data instantly from the app. We do not sell data to third parties.
Can I cancel my subscription?
Yes, anytime — no contract. If you cancel, your account reverts to the free Seed tier. Your historical data and crop records are retained for 12 months in case you resubscribe.
What if the hub breaks?
Replacement hubs ship pre-provisioned. Scan the QR on the new hub, and all your settings, crop records, and historical data are restored from the cloud instantly. No re-setup needed.
